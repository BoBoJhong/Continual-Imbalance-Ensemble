\ncuAbstractPage{\ncuENtitle}{英文摘要}{ABSTRACT}
This study examines a two-stage continual ensemble setting: retaining historical (Old) models and integrating models trained on recent (New) data. Ensemble rules and feature selection are compared. Given the available evidence and experimental configuration, the study evaluates one pool expansion using fixed temporal partitions.

The case study uses U.S. corporate financial records from 1999 to 2018. A retrospective event proxy marks each failed company's last observed year; filing dates are unverified and positives comprise 0.77\%. Four classifiers have event-target results for Old, New, and combined retraining; historical TabM and TabICL scores remain provisional. The framework chooses its base model by validation performance. Current ensemble and feature-selection tests use a six-model XGBoost pool, pending this selection. Four feature-selection methods at two retention levels are evaluated. Ensemble members and thresholds use validation data only.

Recent data benefit classifiers differently. Within the pilot pool, New3 achieves the highest mean AUC (0.8601), while the validation-selected static ensemble achieves the highest F1-score (0.1795). Both improve AUC over equal weighting of all six models after Holm correction across 165 company-cluster paired tests; the static ensemble's F1-score increase does not pass this correction. Dynamic methods do not exceed these respective mean scores, and feature selection yields no consistent improvement. Tuned single classifiers obtain descriptive scores close to or above those of the ensembles, but parameter and validation differences prevent attributing gains to ensembling. Rankings also change with the final-status label, demonstrating that conclusions depend on the target definition.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} continual learning, class imbalance, temporal data, ensemble learning, dynamic selection, feature selection, bankruptcy prediction
