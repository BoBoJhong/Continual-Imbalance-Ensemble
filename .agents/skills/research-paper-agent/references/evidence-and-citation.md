# Evidence and Citation Workflow

## Search protocol

1. Translate the research question into concepts, synonyms, exclusions, and date/venue constraints.
2. Search several complementary routes: scholarly index or search engine, publisher/proceedings site, DOI registry, and backward/forward citation trails.
3. Record the query, date, source, screening rule, and exclusion reason when the work is intended to be systematic or reproducible.
4. Start with seminal/original methods and recent high-quality reviews, then locate the strongest direct evidence for each manuscript claim.

## Verification levels

- **Verified-full**: metadata and supporting passage/table were checked in the full source.
- **Verified-metadata**: bibliographic metadata was checked, but claim-level support still requires full-text verification.
- **Discovery-only**: found in a snippet, bibliography, or secondary summary; do not cite as evidence yet.
- **Unverified**: insufficient access or conflicting metadata; retain only as a documented lead.

## Source assessment

Assess relevance, directness, publication status, venue, methods, sample/data suitability, recency, conflicts, and whether later corrections or retractions exist. “Top-tier” is contextual and is not a substitute for methodological fit.

## Citation binding

For each claim, store:

- exact proposition supported;
- source identifier and stable URL/DOI;
- page, section, figure, table, theorem, or paragraph locator when available;
- verification level and date;
- caveat or conflicting evidence.

One citation need not support every clause in a long sentence. Split compound claims and cite them at the point of support.

## BibTeX quality checks

- Cross-check title, author order, year, venue, volume/issue, pages or article number, and DOI.
- Preserve capitalization that BibTeX might otherwise alter with braces where needed.
- Prefer canonical DOI URLs (`https://doi.org/...`).
- Do not create duplicate records for preprint and published versions without explaining their relationship.
- Never derive a full record solely from another paper's reference list.

## Literature synthesis

Synthesize by research question, mechanism, assumptions, data regime, evaluation protocol, and limitations. Do not produce an author-by-author catalogue when a thematic comparison is possible. Make disagreement and missing evidence visible.
